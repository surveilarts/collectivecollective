Curatorial field notes

Second Skin

A body can be dressed, extended, described, or simulated. Each act changes its appearance. Whether any of them carries the person forward is a much harder question.

This exhibition follows that question through fashion, responsive textiles, a computational double, and an imagined resting chamber. Tailoring becomes a first technology of the self; the final chamber asks what a future technology would need to preserve.

There is no single viewing position. Walk around the garments, linger at the weave, move in front of the double, and return to things you have already seen. Time spent attending to the works leaves small constellations in the final room.

About the works and their responses

All garments, environments, texts, and responsive artworks are original, AI-composed procedural works. The weave responds to the camera’s direction; the double echoes your navigation with a delay. These are authored simulations, not a live AI model, a camera feed, eye tracking, or a brain interface.

The cocoon is a speculative sculpture, not a working life-support or consciousness-transfer device. Your traces exist only in this page’s memory and disappear when it is reloaded. There are no accounts or analytics.

Controls, comfort, and sound

Desktop: drag to look, use WASD to walk, and press E or click the brass sphere to interact. Touch: use the arrow pad to walk and drag the scene to look. “Find your way” provides instant travel to each area.

VR: open over HTTPS in a compatible headset browser. Aim a controller at the floor and pull the trigger to teleport, or select a brass interaction sphere. Without controllers, dwell on a marked destination or sphere. Headset direction stays unchanged after teleporting. Use your headset’s system control to exit.

Pause freezes the moving artworks. You can still walk and make deliberate selections. Reduced-motion preferences start the exhibition paused. Physical pause controls are present at both ends of the nave.

Optional synthesized sound is quiet and changes with your location. Nothing uses your microphone.
